About

I am a PhD student in Measurement, Evaluation, and Statistics at Teachers College, Columbia University. I work on causal inference and missing-data methods for longitudinal and observational data, with applications in education research. I also collaborate with researchers in teacher education, special education, and speech-language pathology, contributing structural equation models, mixed-effects models, and text analysis.

Before my doctorate, I spent nearly eight years as a quantitative researcher at Research for Action, a nonprofit education research organization. I evaluated K–12, higher education, and community programs with state agencies, school districts, and colleges, and served as project director on several projects.

Research

Time-varying treatments

Longitudinal targeted maximum likelihood estimation (TMLE), g-methods, and sequential positivity diagnostics, applied to the effects of special education exposure across the early school years.

Selection bias and estimator performance

Within-study comparisons that benchmark quasi-experimental estimates against experimental results, to learn which covariates and estimators best reduce selection bias.

Missing data

Multiple imputation, inverse probability weighting for attrition, full information maximum likelihood, and sensitivity analysis for missingness assumptions, including how missing-data handling affects downstream analyses.

Program evaluation and costs

Randomized and quasi-experimental evaluations, interrupted time series, and ingredients-method cost-effectiveness analysis of postsecondary, K–12, and community programs.
